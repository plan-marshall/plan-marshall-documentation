[#types]
== The Types
_Requirements: link:../Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-7[PM-ARCH-7]_

The lifecycle of the runtime is plain Java in the package `de.planmarshall.runtime.start` of the library module `pm-runtime` of https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core[`pm-mcp-core`]. It holds no Quarkus type: the assembly `pm-mcp-server` constructs the types and calls them from its `StartupEvent` observer, its signal handlers and its requests (link:assembly-boundary.adoc[The Assembly Boundary]). Why the lifecycle works this way is explained in link:../concepts/runtime-lifecycle.adoc[Concepts § Runtime Lifecycle].

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
|Type |What it does

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/StartupSequence.java[`StartupSequence`]
|Steps 1 to 5 of the start: the singleton lock (`RuntimeLock`), the removal of a stale socket and runtime record under it, the directory check (`BaseDirectoryCheck`), the bounded verification, and a fresh token (`RuntimeTokenFile`). Its outcome is `Ready` (the held lock and the outcome of the audit chain verification), `LockHeld` or `Refused` (exit code `77`).

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/AuditChainVerification.java[`AuditChainVerification`]
|The seam of the bounded verification (step 4): `verify()` answers `Intact`, `Broken(diagnostic)` or `NotVerified(reason)`. On `Broken` the sequence logs `PM_MCP-152`, hands an `audit_chain_broken` record (`category: security`, `outcome: failed`, `details{diagnostic}`) to its `AuditSink`, and goes on. `NONE` answers `NotVerified("no audit chain")`.

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/RuntimeDrain.java[`RuntimeDrain`]
|The drain: `drain()` runs once, every further call waits for the first and gets its `Outcome` (exit code `0`, the participants that got quiet, what was terminated, the failures). `isDraining()` is `true` from the first call on and serves `facts.runtime: draining`. The drain timeout runs on the `Clock` it is given.

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/DrainParticipant.java[`DrainParticipant`]
|The seam of the drain: `stopAdmitting()`, `endIdle()`, `awaitQuiet(deadline)`, `terminateRemaining()` and `close()`, called in that order on every participant.

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/DrainTimeout.java[`DrainTimeout`]
|Reads `drain_timeout_seconds` of `<PM_MCP_BASE>/machine-config.json` with `jackson-core`: `600` when the file or the key is absent, `0` and up as given, and `600` with `PM_MCP-154` for a negative or non-integer value or a file that is unreadable or not one JSON object (link:../user/operator.adoc#drain-timeout-seconds[User Guide § drain_timeout_seconds]).

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/ProjectRecovery.java[`ProjectRecovery`]
|The per-project recovery: `startBackgroundPass()` after the bind, `awaitRecovered(projectId)` for the first call addressing a project, answering `Recovered(outcome)`, `Recovering` after `RECOVERY_WAIT` (10 seconds) or `NotEnrolled`, and `isRecovered(projectId)` for the periodic sweep. The recoveries run on the `Executor` it is given. The constructor with a `backgroundPassTask` submits that task once to the executor after the background pass started the recoveries; the three-argument constructor submits none. `withEnrolment(store, check, enrolments, lockManager, steps, executor)` builds the recovery with the two enrolment steps, `EnrolledProjects.of(store)` and the background task `OwedEnrolmentAuditStep.unenrolledPass()` (link:enrolment-store.adoc#recovery[Enrolment Store § The Recovery]).

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/EnrolledProjects.java[`EnrolledProjects`]
|The seam that lists the enrolled project ids, the population of the recovery and of the sweep. `of(store)` fills it from the enrolment store, a copy of `store.projectIds()` at each call; `NONE` lists none.

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/RecoveryStep.java[`RecoveryStep`]
|The seam of one recovery step: a `Name` (the eleven steps in their order, `ENROLMENT_INTEGRITY` and `OWED_ENROLMENT_AUDIT` first) and `run(projectId)` answering `Done(report)` or `Failed(reason)`. `notImplemented(name)` is the no-op step that reports `not implemented in this runtime`.

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/EnrolmentIntegrityStep.java[`EnrolmentIntegrityStep`]
|Step `ENROLMENT_INTEGRITY`: under the project's enrolment lock, `EnrolmentStore.recoverPair` settles a stopped pair write and `EnrolmentCheck.verify` checks the enrolment; a refusal is a `Failed` with its message (link:enrolment-store.adoc#recovery[Enrolment Store § The Recovery]).

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/OwedEnrolmentAuditStep.java[`OwedEnrolmentAuditStep`]
|Step `OWED_ENROLMENT_AUDIT`: `Enrolments.replayOwedAudit(projectId)` hands over the owed audit records of the project; `unenrolledPass()` is the background task for the projects no longer enrolled (failures logged `PM_MCP-167`).

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/LeaseRecoveryStep.java[`LeaseRecoveryStep`]
|Step `LEASES`: the project-selecting stale lease sweep over every lease store it is given (link:locks-and-leases.adoc#sweep[Locks and Leases § The Stale Lease Sweep]), with the caller's `AbandonmentEvidence`; `AbandonmentEvidence.NONE` finds none, so nothing is released.

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/JournalRollForwardStep.java[`JournalRollForwardStep`]
|Step `JOURNAL_ROLL_FORWARD`: for each scope of `ProjectScopes`, a transaction on the `LockManager` that acquires the scope key and calls `JournalRecovery.recover` (link:store-substrate.adoc[Store Substrate]). An invariant violation, a refused store or an I/O failure is a failure of its scope; the step goes on with the next scope and then fails, listing every failed scope.

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/CrossScopeReconciliationStep.java[`CrossScopeReconciliationStep`]
|Step `CROSS_SCOPE_RECONCILIATION`: every `CrossScopeReconciler` of `ProjectReconcilers` with the `LockManager` and the `AuditSink`; a refused or unreadable store fails the step and the remaining reconcilers do not run.

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/PeriodicSweep.java[`PeriodicSweep`]
|The periodic sweep: `start(executor)` schedules a run every `INTERVAL` (3600 seconds), `runOnce(now)` runs every `SweepStep` for every enrolled project that `recovered` accepts with `olderThan = now − PENDING_ENTRY_MAX_AGE` (86400 seconds) and answers a `Summary` (swept, skipped, failed), and `stop()` cancels the schedule for the drain.

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/SweepStep.java[`SweepStep`]
|The seam of one sweep step: `PENDING_ENTRY_RECORDS` and `FENCED_SESSION_GENERATIONS`, `run(projectId, olderThan)` answering a `RecoveryStep.Outcome`; `notImplemented(name)` is the no-op step.

|https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/main/pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/RelayVersionCheck.java[`RelayVersionCheck`]
|The relay version check: `observe(relayVersion)` with the value of the `PM-MCP-Relay-Version` header (`HEADER`) of every relayed request, and `mismatch()` with the latest `Mismatch` (relay version, runtime version, `pm-operator drain`) for `pm-operator status` alone (link:../user/operator.adoc#relay-version-mismatch[User Guide § Relay Version Mismatch]).
|===

Every log message of the package is a `LogRecord` of `PmMcpLogMessages`, `PM_MCP-53` to `PM_MCP-57`, `PM_MCP-152` to `PM_MCP-159`, `PM_MCP-167` and `PM_MCP-252` (link:../LogMessages.adoc[Log Messages]).

[#seams]
== The Seams
_Requirements: link:../Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-7[PM-ARCH-7]_

Most of what the lifecycle drives does not exist yet: the audit trail, the enrolment store, jobs, workers, the pending store, the task queue. Each is a seam with a default that does nothing harmful, so the lifecycle runs today and a later package fills its part without touching the orchestration:

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
|Seam |Default |Filled by

|`AuditChainVerification`
|`NONE`: no chain to verify
|the audit trail, with the incremental verification of `logs/audit.head`, the rotation index and the `prev_hash` chain

|`DrainParticipant`
|no participant
|jobs, workers, the LSP pool and the web listener, each a participant; the web listener is passed last, so it closes last

|`EnrolledProjects`
|`NONE`: no project
|the enrolment store, through `EnrolledProjects.of(store)`

|`RecoveryStep`
|`notImplemented(name)` for the six steps other than the two enrolment steps, leases, roll-forward and reconciliation; a constructor given no step for an enrolment name uses `notImplemented` for it too
|the package that owns the step's store or component: one `RecoveryStep` with that `Name`

|`LeaseRecoveryStep.AbandonmentEvidence`
|`NONE`: never abandoned
|the stores that know a scope is terminal, archived or gone, a lease expired without adoption, or a slot's job was recorded `killed`

|`JournalRollForwardStep.ProjectScopes`
|`NONE`: no scope
|the per-project machine store: the plan, epic and workspace scopes and the lesson store of a project

|`CrossScopeReconciliationStep.ProjectReconcilers`
|`NONE`: no reconciler
|each store that writes across scopes registers its reconciler

|`SweepStep`
|`notImplemented(name)` for both steps
|the pending store (one audit record per removed record) and the task queue (under the queue lock)
|===

`ProjectRecovery` takes one step per `Name` and refuses two steps with the same name, and a missing name other than the two enrolment steps, which it fills with a seam so that a step list written before the enrolment store keeps working; it sorts them by `Name`, so the order in which they are passed does not matter. A new step is therefore a new `Name` constant at its place in the order of the specification, not a new list position. A step reports a failure as `Failed`, never as an exception; one that throws anyway is recorded as the failed step of its project, so a broken step cannot leave callers waiting.

[#tests]
== Tests
_Requirements: link:../Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-7[PM-ARCH-7], link:../Requirements.adoc#PM-TECH-3[PM-TECH-3]_

The unit tests run on the JVM with fakes and a test clock: `StartupSequenceTest`, `RuntimeDrainTest`, `DrainTimeoutTest`, `ProjectRecoveryTest` (the eleven names in order, a step list without the enrolment steps, the background task, and end to end over a real enrolment store), `EnrolmentIntegrityStepTest`, `OwedEnrolmentAuditStepTest`, `EnrolledProjectsTest`, `LeaseRecoveryStepTest`, `JournalRollForwardStepTest`, `CrossScopeReconciliationStepTest`, `PeriodicSweepTest` and `RelayVersionCheckTest`. The fixtures `ProviderLeaseStores` (a merge-queue and a review-bot lease store, each with a lease, and the assertion that both are byte-identical) and `RecoveryStepFixtures` are shared by them.

*The killed-runtime fixture.* A crash cannot be simulated in the test JVM, because a killed process must really lose its locks and its memory. `KilledRuntimeFixture` starts `RuntimeChildProcess` in a JVM of its own with the `java` and the class path of the test JVM, waits for a named marker line on its standard output, and kills it with `SIGKILL` (`Process.destroyForcibly()`) in the middle of a lifecycle step; the next instance then runs in the test JVM against the same base. Every wait is bounded (`PROCESS_BOUND`, 30 seconds), and the fixture is closed after every test, also a failed one, which kills a child still alive. The child has one mode per step (`startup`, `drain`, `recovery`, `leases`, `rollforward`, `ordered-write`, `sweep`, `version`); a new lifecycle step adds its mode there. The killed-runtime tests run under `-Pintegration-tests`: `BoundedVerificationKilledRuntimeIT`, `RuntimeDrainKilledRuntimeIT`, `ProjectRecoveryKilledRuntimeIT`, `RecoveryStepsKilledRuntimeIT`, `PeriodicSweepKilledRuntimeIT` and `RelayVersionCheckKilledRuntimeIT`. Each test uses its own base from `TestBases`.

*The cold-start guard.* `StartupColdStartIT` runs steps 1 to 5 with the `NONE` audit chain on fresh bases with 0 and with 300 enrolled projects (an enrolment file and a store directory each, written by the test). After five warm-up runs of each population it measures fifteen runs each, alternating, every run on a clean base. The seeded files and directories are forced to disk before the timed region, because step 5 ends in an `fsync` that would otherwise also pay for the seeding's write backlog. The comparison (`ColdStartComparison`) requires the lower quartiles of the two populations to agree within half the larger one plus 5 ms: noise on a shared machine only adds time, so the fast end of each population is the robust measure. A control makes the pass non-vacuous: after a start on a seeded base no seeded file has a new access time, while a file read on purpose has one. The test logs the medians as the JVM figure; on a developer machine both populations had a median of about 5 to 6 ms, agreeing within about 0.5 ms.

[#pitfalls]
== Pitfalls
_Requirements: link:../Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-7[PM-ARCH-7]_

* *Never add work that grows with the projects to the start.* Step 4 reads no enrolment, plan or job file; such work belongs in the per-project recovery or where the file is used. `StartupSequenceTest` and the control of `StartupColdStartIT` fail for a seeded file that is read.
* *Steps 2 to 5 run only under the singleton lock.* Never remove the socket or the runtime record, or write the token, before `RuntimeLock.tryAcquire` has succeeded: a runtime without the lock could unlink the live holder's socket.
* *Decide an admission under the participant's own lock, and re-read `isDraining()` there.* The drain sets its flag before it calls any participant; a participant that checks the flag outside its admission lock can admit a job between the check and the flag, which then outlives the drain unsupervised.
* *Never release a merge-queue or review-bot lease in a drain participant.* A participant releases only its build and model slots and task leases, in `terminateRemaining()`. `RuntimeDrainTest` asserts the provider lease stores byte-identical after a drain.
* *Claim a recovery with `computeIfAbsent`, never with `containsKey` and `put`.* "Recover once, whichever trigger comes first" is a claim on a shared slot; a separate check and act lets the lazy trigger and the background pass both run the steps. Only the installer of the future runs it, and the bounded wait (`Future.get` with a timeout) never cancels it. Across processes the singleton lock rules out two runtimes recovering one base.
* *Decide "record once" by the result of one `Set.add`.* `RelayVersionCheck` writes the audit record of a relay version only from the request whose insert into its concurrent set succeeded; a lookup followed by an insert would let a busy relay write it many times. A refused record removes the version again, so the next request retries.
* *A recovery or sweep step reports, it does not throw.* Return `Failed` with a reason; a `StoreRefusal` or `UncheckedIOException` that escapes is still caught, but loses the report of the step.
* *Select only on positive evidence.* A recovery or sweep step never removes or releases on a name that fails to parse, and excludes the reserved entries of its directory by construction (the sweep rule of `RecoveryStep` and `SweepStep`).
